# Financial statements source snapshot

Source snapshot date: 2026-09-03.

The balance sheet reports assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time, with assets equal to liabilities plus equity. The income statement reports revenue, expenses, and profit over a period. The cash-flow statement reports cash changes from operating, investing, and financing activities over a period. Net income and net cash change are related but not identical.

Double-entry accounting changes at least two accounts for a transaction and keeps total debits equal to total credits. Debit means the left side of an account and credit the right side. Assets and expenses usually increase with debits; liabilities, equity, and revenue usually increase with credits.

Under accrual accounting, revenue and expense recognition follows economic activity rather than cash timing. A credit sale increases revenue and accounts receivable before cash arrives. Later collection increases cash and decreases receivables without recognizing the revenue again.

Buying a machine for cash decreases cash and increases property/equipment. The cash payment is an investing outflow. Because the machine provides benefits over multiple periods, cost enters income through depreciation over those periods rather than becoming a full purchase-period expense. Depreciation reduces profit without being a new cash payment in the depreciation period.
